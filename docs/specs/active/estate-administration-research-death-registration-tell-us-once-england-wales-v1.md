## 2. Executive summary

### Verified findings

- Current legislation and current government guidance place the ordinary registration period at five days from the registrar being notified of medical-examiner confirmation, or from discontinuance of a coroner investigation where applicable. The period includes weekends and bank holidays according to current GOV.UK guidance. This must not be presented as five days from the death.
- A legally qualified informant gives the registration information. The statutory categories are broader and more conditional than “next of kin”; registering a death does not establish authority over the estate.
- Non-coronial deaths are scrutinised by a medical examiner. A coroner investigation changes the ordinary process and can make interim documentation available before final registration.
- Registration is tied nationally to the district where the death occurred, but official local guidance shows appointment and out-of-district practices can vary.
- Tell Us Once is a voluntary DWP-provided service for reporting a death to most government organisations in one interaction. For this dossier, its verified scope is limited to eligible deaths concerning a person living in England or Wales, including a temporary death abroad.
- A registrar may complete Tell Us Once with the user or issue a unique reference for online or telephone use. The official service page states a 28-day service-use period from issue of that reference. This is a service period, not a statutory death-registration deadline.
- Tell Us Once notifies specified government and public-sector bodies. It does not itself settle tax, benefits, pensions, ownership or estate authority.
- GOV.UK explicitly directs users to contact banks, mortgage providers, insurers, utilities, landlords or housing associations, and most personal or workplace pension schemes separately.
- Official privacy material says the service is voluntary, asks for agreement to share, limits transfers to information each recipient needs, and gives the user an opportunity to review recipients before sending. The available privacy statement is dated 30 September 2019 and requires currency review.

### Unresolved questions

The dossier does not establish a universal appointment method, a universal list of mandatory supporting documents, a current national out-of-district workflow, or Tell Us Once coverage for telecom providers, subscriptions, digital accounts, solicitors or funeral directors. The privacy statement’s age, the operational meaning of organisation selection, and the effect of pending electronic-register provisions also require review.

### Product implications that remain proposals

AdminAvenger could later distinguish statutory periods from service windows, show source and jurisdiction beside consequential claims, track a Tell Us Once reference as sensitive data, and separate “notified” from “resolved”. These are proposals, not approved product decisions.

## 6. Death registration findings

### A. Ordinary registration process

- **VF-01 — Verified source fact:** DR-01 requires a death occurring in England or Wales to be registered by the registrar for the sub-district where it occurred. If a body is found and the place of death is unknown, the place where the body was found determines the sub-district.
- **VF-02 — Verified source fact:** DR-03 says all deaths not investigated by a coroner receive medical-examiner scrutiny. DR-02 says the user must wait until the medical examiner’s office confirms registration can proceed.
- **VF-03 — Verified source fact:** DR-01 places the ordinary duty on a qualified informant to provide the required particulars within five days of the “relevant date”. The Act defines that date as registrar notification of medical-examiner confirmation or certification, or discontinuance of a coroner investigation.
- **VF-04 — Verified source fact:** DR-02 states that the five days include weekends and bank holidays and directs a person needing longer to tell the register office immediately.
- **VF-05 — Verified source fact:** DR-01 provides different qualified-informant categories depending on whether the death occurred in a house or elsewhere. Categories include specified relatives or partners, a personal representative, a person present, certain occupiers or residents, a person finding or taking charge of the body, and a person causing disposal of the body. Eligibility and priority are conditional.
- **VF-06 — Local-authority-dependent:** Appointment arrangements are local. Leicester says its registrar contacts the representative after receiving the MCCD; Anglesey says an appointment can then be made; Carmarthenshire allows advance online or telephone booking subject to paperwork checks.
- **VF-07 — Jurisdiction-dependent:** Registration belongs to the district of death, but Carmarthenshire says an out-of-district declaration can sometimes be arranged at another English or Welsh register office and may delay registration and certificates. This is not established as a universal, current workflow.

### B. Information and evidence

- **VF-08 — Local-authority-dependent:** Leicester says the registrar asks for the deceased’s date and place of death; full name, address, date and place of birth; applicable maiden name; occupation; spouse or civil-partner name, date of birth and occupation; NHS or medical-card number; and pension or benefit details.
- **VF-09 — Local-authority-dependent:** Leicester labels passport, driving licence or birth certificate; applicable marriage or civil-partnership certificate; and NHS-number evidence as items to bring “if available”. It says registration can still be completed without them. They must not be described as universal legal requirements.
- **VF-10 — Local-authority-dependent:** Carmarthenshire says the person attending needs a form of personal identification and lists information about the deceased. This establishes a Carmarthenshire instruction, not a national evidence rule.
- **VF-11 — Product inference requiring approval:** Accurate source information may help avoid correction, delay or fees, but AdminAvenger must not infer that every item requested by a local page is mandatory or that the same evidence list applies everywhere.

### C. Outputs from registration

- **VF-12 — Verified source fact:** DR-02 says the user receives a certificate for burial or cremation, often called the green form, and must give it to the funeral director, crematorium or burial authority before the funeral.
- **VF-13 — Verified source fact:** DR-03 describes a death certificate as a certified copy of the register entry that the informant can buy after registration. This is distinct from the MCCD and from the burial-or-cremation form.
- **VF-14 — Local-authority-dependent:** Leicester describes a funeral-director form and offers death certificates for purchase. Carmarthenshire describes a full copy of the register entry for purchase. Prices and exact local issue arrangements are mutable and are not proposed as product truth.
- **VF-15 — Local-authority-dependent:** Leicester says a Tell Us Once reference number is received at registration. National TUO-01 is more careful: the registrar may complete the service with the user or give a unique reference. The national formulation should control.

### D. Exceptional or altered processes

- **VF-16 — Verified source fact:** Under DR-04, if the coroner does not investigate, the medical examiner confirms registration can proceed; if the coroner finds the cause clear, the coroner sends a certificate to the registrar and the registrar arranges registration.
- **VF-17 — Verified source fact:** DR-04 says an interim death certificate can be requested while an inquest is pending and a final death certificate can be obtained from the registrar after the inquest. Either can support Tell Us Once according to that page.
- **VF-18 — Verified source fact:** DR-01 provides a narrow legal route in which qualifying written notice, accompanied by the specified medical certificate and sent within five days, allows the registration particulars to be provided within fourteen days of the relevant date. This exception needs legal review before any product use.
- **VF-19 — Jurisdiction-dependent:** DR-06 says a death abroad must be registered with authorities in the country where it occurred. Optional UK recording and repatriation steps are a separate process and are out of scope here.
- **VF-20 — Local-authority-dependent:** Anglesey describes a nine-day aim for completion of death certification, subject to factors including records access, family contact, seasonal demand and coroner investigation. This is a local typical-processing aim, not a legal deadline.
- **VF-21 — Prohibited conclusion:** Stillbirth, an unidentified person and missing-person cases have distinct official routes. This dossier does not establish those routes and they must not be folded into the ordinary death-registration flow.

## 7. Tell Us Once findings

### A. Purpose

- **VF-22 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 describes Tell Us Once as a service for reporting a death to most government organisations in one interaction.
- **VF-23 — Verified source fact:** TUO-03 says DWP provides the service on behalf of government and describes it as voluntary. It is not mandatory.
- **VF-24 — Jurisdiction-dependent:** For this dossier, use is verified where the deceased lived in England or Wales. TUO-01 also permits a temporary death abroad. It excludes a person living in Northern Ireland or permanently abroad. Scotland appears only as an explicit boundary and is not researched.

### B. Access

- **VF-25 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 requires the death to be registered, or reported to a coroner with a final or interim death certificate available.
- **VF-26 — Verified source fact:** A registrar explains Tell Us Once and either completes it with the user or provides a unique reference for online or telephone use. An inquest case can obtain a reference after an interim death certificate is obtained.
- **VF-27 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 states that the service must be used within 28 days of receiving the unique reference. This is classified as a service-use period, not a statutory deadline.
- **VF-28 — Verified source fact:** The reference, deceased’s surname and date of death are among the information needed. Depending on circumstances, the service asks for estate-contact, surviving spouse or civil-partner, next-of-kin, institution, passport, driving, vehicle, council, benefits and pension information.

### C. Organisations covered

- **VF-29 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 explicitly lists HMRC, DWP, HM Passport Office, DVLA, the local council and Veterans UK.
- **VF-30 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 identifies covered local-council purposes: Housing Benefit, Council Tax Reduction or Support, Blue Badge, council housing and electoral-register records.
- **VF-31 — Conditional:** TUO-01 lists certain public-sector or protection-fund pension schemes. Coverage depends on the deceased having the relevant scheme record.
- **VF-32 — Verified source fact:** HMRC and DWP may contact the user after notification about tax, benefits and entitlements. Notification is not evidence that those matters are resolved.

### D. Matters not covered

- **VF-33 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 explicitly directs users to contact banks, mortgage providers, insurance providers, utilities, landlords or housing associations, and personal or workplace pension schemes other than listed public schemes.
- **VF-34 — Verified source fact:** Additional separate action may be needed for property records, some vehicle actions, HMRC business taxes, the Student Loans Company and a parent’s or carer’s benefits after a child’s death.
- **VF-35 — Unresolved:** Coverage for building societies is not named separately; it may fall within financial accounts but that is not established. Telecom providers, subscriptions, digital accounts, solicitors and funeral directors are also not established. Silence is not a “No”.
- **VF-36 — Verified source fact:** If Tell Us Once is not used, TUO-01 and TUO-02 direct the person to notify relevant organisations themselves.

### E. Privacy and data sharing

- **VF-37 — Verified source fact:** TUO-03 says the service is voluntary, asks the user to agree to sharing, and asks whether the user has another person’s agreement when providing that person’s information. TUO-01 specifically requires permission before providing listed third-party details.
- **VF-38 — Verified source fact:** TUO-03 says only information needed by each recipient is passed to it and the user has an opportunity to check information and recipients before sending.
- **VF-39 — Verified source fact with currency risk:** TUO-03 identifies DWP as controller, says the service meets government security standards, describes time-limited deletion in non-specific terms, excludes marketing and unspecified third parties, and provides a contact route. These are official statements, not an AdminAvenger guarantee; the 2019 date requires privacy review.
- **VF-40 — Verified source fact:** TUO-01 provides telephone accessibility through Relay UK and BSL video relay. TUO-04 provides Easy Read in English and Welsh and an accessible-format request route.

## 14. Claims that must not enter the product

- Registering the death establishes authority over the estate.
- The person registering is automatically the executor, administrator, personal representative or next of kin.
- A registrar appoints an executor or administrator or decides whether probate is required.
- A Tell Us Once reference proves identity, entitlement or estate authority.
- Tell Us Once completes all notifications, contacts every organisation or applies throughout the UK.
- Tell Us Once contacts banks, building societies, mortgage providers, private insurers, utilities, telecom providers, landlords, subscriptions, digital accounts, solicitors or funeral directors without specific current evidence.
- Tell Us Once resolves tax, benefits, pensions, Council Tax, vehicle ownership or account closure.
- Every government body is notified automatically, without relevant records, information, consent or service conditions.
- Every death follows the same registration process or produces the same documents.
- Every document mentioned by a council is legally mandatory.
- The MCCD, death certificate, interim death certificate and green form are interchangeable.
- The five-day period starts on the date of death.
- The 28-day Tell Us Once period is statutory.
- The Anglesey nine-day aim is a guarantee or national deadline.
- Coroner involvement always means an inquest, post-mortem or wrongdoing.
- “Next of kin” alone confers legal decision-making or estate authority.
- An official source’s silence proves non-coverage.
- A privacy statement means AdminAvenger may collect or retain the same data.
- The 2019 Tell Us Once privacy statement is necessarily current in every operational detail.
- Registration, notification or possession of a certificate proves benefit, tax, debt, pension or inheritance entitlement.

## 15. Product implications

### Source-supported implications

- Consequential claims need visible source, jurisdiction, access/review date and classification.
- Dates should preserve their event type: date of death, registrar notification/confirmation, appointment date and reference-issue date are not interchangeable.
- A workspace could represent register office, medical examiner, coroner and Tell Us Once as distinct organisation or process families.
- A Tell Us Once reference is a sensitive service credential, not a status or authority token.
- Information requested by a registrar must remain separate from evidence the user is legally required to produce.
- Organisation status needs at least “notified”, “follow-up expected”, “separate action” and “not established”; “resolved” cannot be inferred.

### Product proposals requiring approval

- A source-grounded checklist could say “check locally” for appointments and documents.
- A timing card could explain the statutory start event and separately label the 28-day service period.
- A notification matrix could be populated only from the current official TUO list and reviewed on a scheduled trigger.
- “Have ready” prompts could be conditional and avoid storing values unless the user chooses.
- Timeline events could record confirmation received, registration appointment, registration complete and Tell Us Once submitted without implying estate progress.
- A calm escalation could direct the user to the register office where the relevant date, qualification or exception is unclear.

No proposal in this section is an implementation decision.

## 20. Privacy and emotional-safety implications

### Official evidence

- TUO-03 supports voluntary participation, consent, limited recipient data, pre-send review, DWP control and stated security measures.
- TUO-01 requires permission before providing specified third-party information.
- TUO-01 requests sensitive identifiers only where relevant, including National Insurance, passport, driving-licence, vehicle and Blue Badge data.
- TUO-04 and TUO-01 evidence Easy Read, Welsh, Relay UK, BSL video relay and accessible-format routes.
- Anglesey says bereavement can be distressing and that people experience grief differently. Carmarthenshire acknowledges difficulty without prescribing an emotional response.

### Product design judgements requiring approval

- Collect the minimum needed for a user-chosen task; do not mirror every TUO field into AdminAvenger.
- Avoid collecting another living person’s details merely because the deceased had a spouse, partner or relative.
- Preserve the user’s wording for the deceased person’s name and relationship; do not force “next of kin”.
- Treat the Tell Us Once reference and identifiers as sensitive, mask them where possible, and avoid logs or analytics.
- Place the current official privacy notice at the decision point if a future product links to the service.
- Use calm, direct wording. Avoid “simple”, “just”, urgency theatre, grief assumptions or congratulatory completion language.
- Make pause, skip and return options visible. Do not interpret delay as failure.
- Never infer legal authority, family hierarchy, consent or emotional state from relationship data.

These design judgements are not claims about DWP processing and are not approved.

## 23. Research conclusion

Authoritative sources verify the ordinary England and Wales registration baseline, the post-2024 medical-examiner role, qualified-informant categories, the five-day statutory period and its starting event, limited coroner and overseas exceptions, principal registration outputs, and the core Tell Us Once eligibility, access, organisation, separate-action and privacy statements.

Local appointment and evidence practice, current out-of-district mechanics, several private-sector coverage questions, privacy-notice currency, retention, granular recipient selection and electronic-register effects remain unresolved.

After human approval, it appears safe to consider source-linked, jurisdiction-qualified explanations of the ordinary process; careful distinction between legal and service periods; local-office handoffs; a conditional Tell Us Once coverage matrix; and a strict “notified is not resolved” status model. It is not safe to imply estate authority, universal coverage, universal documents, entitlement, resolution or privacy guarantees.

This dossier is ready for human review. It is not approved for product use or implementation, and it authorises no implementation work.
